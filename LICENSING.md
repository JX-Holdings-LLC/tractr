# Licensing

Tractr is free to use until a game you make with it earns US$1,000,000. This

LICENSE is the one that counts.

## What does it cost?

| Your situation | Cost |
|---|---|
| Learning, hobby projects, jams, school, free games | Nothing |
| A game that has earned less than US$1,000,000 | Nothing |
| A game that reaches US$1,000,000 | A commercial license for that game |

- **The limit is per game.** A studio with ten games pays only for the ones
  that pass US$1,000,000, and not for the rest.
- **Revenue is the game's lifetime gross.** It counts sales, subscriptions,
  in-game purchases, ads, sponsorships and money raised for that game, before
  the platform's cut, and not sales tax, VAT or refunds. Ports, updates,
  expansions and DLC of a game count toward the same game.
- **Commercial licenses are a flat fee per game, not a share of sales.** You
  have 60 days after a game reaches the limit to get one, and can keep selling
  meanwhile. To get one, contact JX Holdings, LLC through
  <https://github.com/JX-Holdings-LLC>.

## Who owns and ships what?

- **Your game is yours.** The project, art, text, sounds and plugins you make
  belong to you, and you pick the terms you sell it under.
- **Players need nothing.** Anyone can play your exported game. The player and
  runtime inside it are yours to distribute with the game, as long as the
  licenses folder the exporter writes ships with it.
- **Built-in art and the sample farm** may be used in your games.

## Plugins, packs and mods

Plugins and content packs (the JavaScript and data that the plugin sandbox
runs) are their authors' own works, not parts of Tractr. You can share or sell
them on any terms without a license from JX Holdings, LLC. A pack's sales are
not added to the games that load it, and a game's sales are not added to its
packs. A plugin or pack that is itself a game follows the same rule as any
other game.

A plugin only counts as separate if it does not copy Tractr's code into
itself.

## Forks and modified versions

You may fork Tractr, change it and share your changes. A fork is still Tractr
for licensing: it keeps this license, and games made with it follow the same
per-game limit. What you may not do without a commercial license is sell or
host Tractr, or a fork, as a game-making product or service.

## Versions and old releases

- **Each version has its own license.** A later license never takes away what
  the one a version shipped with gave you.
- **Releases published before 2026-10-23 were under the MIT License** and stay
  that way for anyone who has them.
- **Every version becomes MIT after four years.** The grant for a version
  ends and the MIT License applies from that version's fourth anniversary.
